### Clean read-aloud transcript

**H1 — Introduction.** What if your workflows could learn, prove their work, and recover when reality changes? I'm Harikeshav Rameshkumar, from The Ohio State University. I built Oryn for the IBM AI Builders Challenge, North America, AI for Business. Oryn turns how people work into governed agents that can be tested, supervised, and improved. The standard is not whether an agent clicked. It is whether the business result is right.

**H2 — Problem.** Accounts payable teams move invoices between inboxes, purchase orders, spreadsheets, and messages. Leaders inherit exceptions. Ardent Partners reports an average invoice costs nine dollars and ninety cents to process and takes eleven point six days. Automation can complete every click and post the wrong result. When a supplier changes its invoice format, a brittle workflow may fail without explaining why. People are left to investigate.

**H3 — Solution, AI and IBM Bob.** I built a closed loop: observe, understand, review, publish, compile, simulate, authorize, execute, verify, diagnose, correct, repair, re-evaluate, and release. Private evidence, exclusions, and masking stay local. Authorized AI inference travels through a governed cloud gateway. AI helps discover workflows, draft agents, generate scenarios, assist verification, and propose repairs; people retain authority. I used IBM Bob to build and review the prototype. Here is the review session and its outcome.

**H4 — Demo.** Priya enrolls, sees the notice, chooses scope, and can pause. With meeting consent, Oryn proposes a commitment; Priya corrects and confirms it. Three demonstrations become a candidate, not policy. Marcus reviews evidence and publishes the playbook. I create a scoped agent with explicit stopping points. Stage three requires verified examples, representative tests, and owner approval. The simulator catches a boundary error: exactly two percent should be accepted. I correct it and rerun the suite. Now eleven invoices enter a supervised job. Seven are verified, two escalated, one needs approval, and one is unknown. Daniel reviews the variance and approves; the unknown submission stays held, never blindly retried. Next, a supplier changes its layout. Oryn contains three affected cases before any repair. Elena explains the handling fee; Marcus checks the scope. The correction is evidence, not new policy. A bounded repair passes regression tests, enters limited release, and expands after three verified outcomes. The operations map connects the workflow, rules, agent, and incident. Policy changes trigger revalidation; unsafe actions stop for approval. The command center keeps exceptions visible.

**H5 — Impact and next steps.** The aim is fewer repeated investigations and clearer, verifiable decisions, not a dashboard of assumed success. Next I will deepen supported application validation, evidence-backed verification, and controlled releases. Later directions include optional on-device inference. I'm building Oryn so operational knowledge can improve without surrendering human control. Explore the project on GitHub.

## Appendix B — Project-page copy

### Problem

Operational procedures are scattered across inboxes, enterprise applications, spreadsheets, conversations and the experience of the people doing the work. Accounts payable specialists must connect an invoice to its purchase order, interpret the right rule and resolve exceptions; operations leaders need a clear view of what happened. A conventional automation may complete every click while leaving the wrong business result. Small changes in a supplier's document format can break an otherwise familiar process. Ardent Partners reports an average $9.90 and 11.6 days to process an invoice, and 19.9% requiring manual exception handling. These are industry context, not results attributed to Oryn.

### Challenge

I built Oryn for the IBM AI Builders Challenge, North America, in the AI for Business track. The challenge I chose is making operational knowledge useful without confusing observed behavior with policy or completed clicks with correct outcomes. A workflow needs a human owner, evidence, explicit permissions, representative tests and a way to handle uncertainty. An agent should not gain blanket authority because someone started a job. A financial exception should reach the appropriate approver, and an uncertain external submission should stay held until reconciled. The reference journey follows supplier invoice versus purchase order reconciliation, from employee demonstrations to controlled agent repair.

### AI and technical approach

Oryn organizes a governed loop from permitted observation through workflow discovery, review, publication, agent definition, simulation, authorization, execution and independent outcome verification. Failures create incidents that can lead to human corrections, bounded repair proposals, regression tests and controlled releases. Deterministic capture, exclusions, protected-field masking and private evidence storage stay local. Authorized AI inference uses approved cloud models through an Oryn-managed LiteLLM gateway; v1.0 does not use on-device model inference. Cloud AI assists with discovery, agent definitions, scenario generation, verification and repair proposals. Model output is not authorization to publish a policy, approve a payment or broaden an agent's scope.

### Impact

Oryn is designed to help teams preserve useful procedural knowledge, reduce repeated investigation and distinguish verified results from cases that need attention. In the illustrated job, seven cases are Verified Complete, two are Escalated for missing purchase orders, one requires approval and one remains Outcome Unknown. After Daniel approves the consequential variance and the outcome is verified, eight are Verified Complete; the unknown case is still held. These numbers describe the demonstrated job, not a measured customer outcome or savings estimate. The intended benefit is a more accountable operational process: evidence for decisions, visible exceptions and a clear path from correction to reviewed improvement.

### Next steps

My next priorities are deeper application-specific validation, clear evidence-backed outcome verification and the controls needed for safe, limited releases. The v1.0 scope includes employee privacy controls, reviewable playbooks, a simulator, supervised execution, incident handling, version history and auditability. Each supported application and action needs an explicit capability and authorization boundary; unsupported operations must remain unavailable. Future directions include optional on-device inference, which is not part of the v1.0 cloud inference design. I also want to strengthen regression coverage and make the relationship among workflows, rules, agents, incidents and releases easier for an authorized owner to inspect.
